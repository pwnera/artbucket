## Share links

**Share** on a collection or an asset makes a link to look and download, at
`/s/{token}`: approved assets only, and the current version of each.
**Request uploads** makes a link anyone can send files through, a photographer
or an agency: what they send lands `proposed`, for review.

Either can end on a date and ask for a password, and revoking one stops it at
once. Making one takes write on what it shares.

```bash
artbucket share {collection-id} --password dragon --expires 2027-01-31
artbucket share {collection-id} --upload --name "Photographer drop"
```

## One asset

Nothing leaves the team until someone sends it out. **Share** on an asset
offers four ways out:

- **Team**: its link in the library, for people who can see it. Nobody else
  gets in.
- **Share link**: a page for it, as above, with a password and an end date if
  you like.
- **Signed URL**: the file itself, for anyone who has the URL, for an hour to a
  year. Add a rendition before the query (`/a/{id}/w_800,f_webp?s=...`).
- **Embed**: the file for anyone, until turned off, to embed on a site, in a doc or
  in an email. It shows two URLs: `/c/{id}`, always the current version, the
  one to embed, and `/a/{id}`, this version only
  ([delivery](/guides/assets#delivery)). Turning it off stops both.

**Sizes and formats**, on an approved asset, copies links that follow new
versions (`/c/`) unless you switch that off; a link signed for anyone names
this version only.

Each takes share on the asset, and works only while it is approved and
unexpired: archiving it stops all of them at once. Share links and portals
sign the files they show a day at a time, so revoking one stops its files
within a day.

```bash
curl -X POST https://assets.example.com/api/v1/assets/{id}/signed-url \
  -H "authorization: Bearer $KEY" -H 'content-type: application/json' \
  -d '{"expiresIn": 604800}'
```

<Note>
For something that stays up (a press kit, a partner hub), make a
[portal](/guides/portals): several collections, your look, search, and
downloads sized for the job.
</Note>

## People and access

An **organization** is a team; a **project** is a library of its own inside
one. What a person may do is their **grants**: a scope (`read`, `propose`,
`write`, `admin`) on the organization, a project, a collection or one asset.
Grants add up and reach down: admin on the organization is admin everywhere in
it, write on a collection is write on its assets. Roles are one ladder, with
nothing switched off per grant: an editor edits, approves, deletes and shares;
someone who should only suggest is a contributor. [Who sees what](/guides/access)
draws it out, with what **Who sees it** on an asset changes.

Invite people on Team: an email and a scope on something, and a link that
works once, for a week.

## Projects and organizations

Settings, Organization, Projects makes, renames and deletes projects;
Settings, Organization, General deletes the organization. Both are for its
admins, both ask for the name to be typed, and neither can be undone: the
project's assets, collections, brands, keys and links go at once, and its
files at the next sweep. An organization keeps at least one project, and a
server at least one organization.
