Brand rules are records, not a PDF: a dotted key, a typed value (`color`,
`text`, `number`, `list` or `font`), a sentence on how to use it, and the assets
it points at.

In the app, open the brand under **Brands**: its **Rules** tab holds every
rule, and **Release** in its header puts them in front of readers. A new brand
opens on its Overview, whose next steps say what to add. Scripts and agents
write the same records:

```bash
artbucket rules set color.primary '#34a853' --type color --usage "Buttons, links, the mark's tile"
artbucket rules set color.primary '#5bc27a' --type color --context dark-background
curl 'https://assets.example.com/api/v1/brand/rules?context=dark-background'
```

A rule can be scoped to a context. Asking for one returns one rule per key:
the context's own where it has one, the default otherwise.

## The guidelines page

Each brand, under Brands in the sidebar, opens on its page, a repository's:
the header names it (its mark, whether its organization is verified, whether
it is public on BrandHub, a link to its page there, `{org}/{brand}`, what is live and whether it is
the latest: "@4 live · Unreleased changes", "@4 live · Up to date" or "Never
released", where it is live, "@4 live on BrandHub, Press and Partners" or "Only the team", each place a
link out in a popover (portals only for whoever manages them), and the
release's note), with **Use this brand** (the MCP server, `brand.json` and `llms.txt` on
BrandHub, the tokens, `DESIGN.md`, the [Office templates](#office-templates) once released, and a copy as a new brand), **Edit** (the
three ways to change the pages, [below](#brand-pages)) and, while readers don't see the latest, **Release** beside it, the one
filled button; the rest is in the brand's menu at the header's end (Theme, Insights, Settings,
Connect Git, pinning it to the sidebar, opening it in the catalog). Every tab stays in the page,
under the same header (folded to one
line over Guidelines, the tabs inline or, when narrow, in a menu on the
brand's name, so the pages get the screen). Six tabs, one job each, in the order a brand is made: Overview,
Rules (its rules by section, edited in place by whoever may edit the brand,
dragged into the order its pages show them or moved and deleted from each
rule's menu, and read by everyone else; its
tokens in every format above them, at `/brands/{slug}/rules`), Assets (the files its rules hold and its
pages show, at `/brands/{slug}/assets`, from `GET /api/v1/brands/{slug}/assets`),
Guidelines (the brand's pages as readers see them, at `/brands/{slug}/guidelines`:
the live release, or the draft to whoever may edit it while it has
unreleased changes, always labeled, with a switch between them),
Releases, Sharing (who reads it outside the team, at `/brands/{slug}/sharing`:
BrandHub, public or private, the portals showing it with the one BrandHub
links as its guidelines, and the addresses agents and code read). In the menu:
Theme (the look of its pages, at `/brands/{slug}/theme`), Insights (at `/brands/{slug}/insights`;
its summary is the Overview's Health) and Settings (its name, address and domain,
the default, its repository, deleting it, at `/brands/{slug}/settings`). The Overview says what the brand is (its line, the usage terms of
the portal BrandHub links, the BrandHub brand it started from), its colors,
its latest release with what it changed, its typefaces, and its Health: the
Brand Agent Score and the signals that say it is read, each opening its own page. Its
guidelines (`?focus=1` on the reader shows the pages on their own) are drawn
from those records, and the records are edited in place on the Rules tab:
click a value to change it, press `/` to add a rule from named building blocks
(brand color, clear space, logo don'ts, type scale, words to avoid), give it
context versions, a spec and files, drag to reorder or use a rule's menu (Move
up, Move down, Duplicate, Delete, with Undo). `/brands/{slug}/rules#rule-{key}`
opens that rule there. Colors are a table, by their spec's group: each token's
light value beside its dark one (its `dark-background` version, or the brand's own
`dark` context; **Inherits Light** without one, and **A dark value** in its
details adds it). Text colors (`ink`, `muted`, `nightInk`, or a color's `pair`)
show their contrast on their group's ground (`paper`, `surface`, `night`) in
both themes. Opened, a color shows its contrast on white and black; a numeric `type.scale`
renders as a specimen. An attached asset can name a rendition (`w_512,f_png`):
agents then get that exact URL.

## Brand pages

**Edit**, in the brand's header and on the Guidelines tab, opens the three
ways to change the pages, over the pages as readers see them
(`/brands/{slug}/guidelines?ways=1`): an agent over
[MCP](/developers/mcp#brand-pages), with the pages tools (`list_pages`,
`get_page`, `save_page`, `edit_page`, `generate_pages`, `delete_page`,
`preview_page`, `list_templates`), Git ([brand as code](/guides/brand-as-code)),
or [Eject to code](/developers/kit#eject-to-code). The pages API and MCP write
the same pages. The rules are edited on the **Rules** tab and the look on
**Theme** (`/brands/{slug}/theme`).

A brand with no pages gets them laid out from its rules by `generate_pages`,
or `POST /api/v1/brands/{slug}/pages`: `color.primary`, `color.secondary`,
`color.dark`, `type.heading`, `type.body`, `logo.primary` and `tone.voice`
make the cover and the pages after it, and a brand with none of them starts
from a cover. Set them on the **Rules** tab, like any other. The score, of 100, weighs the brand's steps to being worth sharing (colors,
typefaces, logo, voice, a look, pages, a release, a portal) by what each gives
an agent working from it; the brand's Overview shows it, and
its own page (`/brands/{slug}/score`, from the Overview) lists every step with
what it gives, the two fixes worth the most, a prompt that hands them to an
agent connected over MCP, and BrandHub's public score for the domain the
organization proved. Agents read the same
score and steps with `brand_status`, or `GET /api/v1/brands/{slug}/status`.

Review comments on the pages are read and answered on the Guidelines tab:
**Comments** in its bar opens them for the page on show (`?comments=1`), with
the other pages that have open threads one click away. Agents read and write
them over MCP (`list_comments`, `add_comment`, `update_comment`,
`delete_comment`).

An embed section takes a link: a YouTube, Vimeo or Loom page becomes its
player. Readers see a placeholder naming the service until they click to load
it, so their browser contacts YouTube, Vimeo, Loom, Figma or Google only when
they choose.

The rules are the content; pages lay them out for people. A brand's pages
form a tree, up to three levels deep (Overview, then Identity with Color, Logo
and Type under it), and each page is sections built from templates: a cover,
a palette, type specimens, logo rules, do's and don'ts, a gallery, a live
collection, an icon set (what the library tags `icon`, which an icon pack
import tags). A section shows rules by key and never copies a value, so change
`color.primary` and every page that shows it follows.

```bash
artbucket templates                  # what each template shows and binds
artbucket pages generate             # a first layout from the rules
artbucket page save color color.json # make or replace a page whole
artbucket page color                 # a page as Markdown
```

A page reads as a chapter of the book (`layout: book`, the default: the nav
beside it, on-this-page, and the next page at its foot) or as a front
(`layout: landing`: none of those, for a home or a campaign page). Its
`audience` says who may read it on a portal, and `hidden` keeps it from
readers altogether. The theme (`/api/v1/brands/{slug}/theme`) says which rules
play which part (accent, grounds, ink, faces, logo) and sets the measure,
navigation and on-this-page for every page. Agents build all of it over
[MCP](/developers/mcp#brand-pages).

## Fonts

A font rule's files are its typeface: pages set the face in them, served from
`/a/` like any asset, and readers' browsers call no one else. A rule whose
`spec.source` is `google` gets its files from Google Fonts on the server: when
it is written without any (the brand's setup, a template, the rule editor,
`set_rules`), and again before each release. Brands from before the server did
this get theirs from its sweep, at start and every six hours, a batch at a
time: the files go onto the draft's rules and into every release's stored
rules, so what readers see keeps its look, with no new version. The files are
the family's every weight and italic, in the library, as **Import from Google
Fonts**, `import_google_font` and `POST /api/v1/fonts/google` make them;
brought in with a key that may only propose, they wait for approval like
anything it adds. A rule still without files (Google out of reach, someone who
can't add to the library, a brand kept in Git, whose files are its
repository's to hold) is set in its `spec.fallback`. For a brand kept in Git
the sweep patches its releases only, and its next push releases what the
repository holds. Only the font picker and a brand's setup, in the app, show
families straight from Google while someone chooses.

## Releasing

Every edit to the rules, the pages or the theme is a draft. Releasing makes
the brand as it stands the version [portals](/guides/portals) show, until the
next release. The command and the endpoint are still called `publish`:

```bash
artbucket publish --note "A warmer primary, and a page on motion"
curl -X POST https://assets.example.com/api/v1/brands/default/publish \
  -H 'Content-Type: application/json' -d '{"note": "A warmer primary"}'
```

The note (and an optional `image` beside it) is what readers see in What's
new, with what the release changed since the one before: rules and pages
added, changed and removed. The answer names the portals showing the brand.
Releasing with nothing changed does nothing. It takes Editor on the brand.

A release is numbered as its version (`@5`). The
brand's **Releases** tab (`/brands/{slug}/releases`) lists them, each with its
note and what it changed; **Release** in the brand's header opens the form (`/brands/{slug}/releases/new`): the
note, what changes since the last release line by line (a color's value
before and after, a page's sections edited), and the Brand Agent Score before
and after. While only the team reads the brand (no portal shows it, and it is
private on BrandHub), the form asks who should see it: a portal of its own,
open to the project's members or to anyone, for whoever manages portals,
and **Public on BrandHub** for whoever may release the brand,
applied once the release lands.

## Design tokens

`GET /api/v1/brand/tokens?format=css` is the brand as CSS custom properties
with `@font-face`; `format=json` is W3C design tokens; `format=designmd` is a
[DESIGN.md](https://github.com/google-labs-code/design.md) for coding agents,
the tokens with the brand's guidance as prose, to keep at a repository's root.
[Standards](/guides/brand-as-code/standards) has every format and how rules
become each.

## Office templates

A release is also a PowerPoint template (`.potx`) and a Word template
(`.dotx`), for the people who make decks and documents rather than code.
On Artbucket Cloud from Team; every self-hosted install has it. A server that
sells plans leaves `templates` out of a plan's [features](/configuration/limits)
to turn them off, your own templates below and a portal's included; BrandHub's
press kit stays.
Members get them on **Templates**, in the sidebar, with what each holds and
the brand's share card and press kit, and under **Use this brand**; portal
visitors beside the logo kit, on a logos section that offers it.

```bash
curl -OJ "https://assets.example.com/api/v1/brand/office?brand=acme&format=potx"
curl -OJ "https://assets.example.com/api/v1/brand/office?brand=acme&format=dotx&version=4"
```

Without `version`, the release readers see; a brand edited and never released
has no template. On a portal, `GET /api/v1/portal/{slug}/brands/{brand}/office?format=potx`,
behind the portal's door.

Both carry the brand as an Office theme, the one PowerPoint and Word list
under Design and Page Layout:

| Office slot | From the release |
|---|---|
| Text/Background dark 1, light 1 (`dk1`, `lt1`) | The ink and the paper, as the brand's pages wear them ([Theme](/guides/portals#look)) |
| Dark 2, light 2 (`dk2`, `lt2`) | The dark color and the panel; on a dark paper, the panel and the quiet text |
| Accent 1 | The accent |
| Accents 2 to 6 | The palette's other colors, in its order, then tints and shades of the accent |
| Hyperlink, followed hyperlink | The accent as text, the quiet text |
| Headings, body fonts | The heading and text faces |

The deck has a slide master with three layouts (title, title and content,
section on the accent) and opens on a title slide with the brand's name. The
document has Title and Heading 1 to 3 in the heading face, body text in the
text face, on A4. The logo, the theme's logo rule's first file, sits on the
master, large on the title layout and in the document's header, drawn as a
PNG; a logo [shown only](/guides/assets#shown-not-handed-out) stays out.

Office can't load a web font from a template, so the theme names each family
and says what kind of face it is (sans, serif or mono, read from its
fallback): where the family isn't installed, Office sets a face of that kind,
and Word the fallback's first named family. Install the brand's fonts for the
real thing; the typography section offers them.

Google Slides opens a `.potx` uploaded to Drive (Open with, Google Slides),
and LibreOffice Impress opens one too: there is no Slides integration to set up. One
release is one file byte for byte, so a download sends an `ETag` and the same
copy answers `304`.

### Your own templates

A deck an agency designed, a letterhead, a budget sheet: upload the
PowerPoint, Word or Excel file (`.potx`, `.pptx`, `.dotx`, `.docx`, `.xltx`,
`.xlsx`) to the library and put it on one of the brand's rules, as a logo is.
A `templates.slides` rule that says "Start every deck from this one" is the
usual place. Once released, it is listed on **Templates** beside the generated
ones, and each download wears that release:

- every theme part takes the release's colors and its heading and text
  families, as in the table above; script faces (Japanese, Arabic...) stay
- each image the library has, matched by its bytes, is replaced by that
  asset's current version: a new logo reaches every deck without anyone
  opening it. A replacement in another format is drawn as the image's own
- nothing else changes: the layouts, placeholders, text and effects stay as
  the designer made them

The file itself isn't rewritten: like the generated ones, each download is
made from the release it asks for, so an older release still gives its own.
The file has to be approved, usable now and free to leave the team, and the
caller has to be able to see it; a new version of the file takes over as soon
as it is current. An image is replaced only by a version that may leave too.
On the brand's pages, the file's tile on its rule downloads it this way, and
portal visitors get it behind the portal's door
(`GET /api/v1/portal/{slug}/brands/{brand}/office/templates/{id}`).

```bash
curl "https://assets.example.com/api/v1/brand/office/templates?brand=acme"
curl -OJ "https://assets.example.com/api/v1/brand/office/templates/{id}?brand=acme"
```

The list says, for each file, what won't follow a release: colors and fonts
written on shapes and text instead of taken from the theme (a color of the
brand's palette counts as the brand's), images the library doesn't have, and
a file with no theme at all. `follows` is true when there is none of that.
Ask the designer to use the theme's colors and fonts (Design, Variants in
PowerPoint; Design, Colors and Fonts in Word) and the library's logo.

## Brands and history

A project can hold several brands, one of them the default. Every change is
kept, like a shared doc: edits close together are one version, a named version
is a checkpoint, and any version can be restored, as a new version. Released
versions are marked; a restore is a draft like any edit, until it is
released.

```bash
curl https://assets.example.com/api/v1/brands/default/versions
curl -X POST https://assets.example.com/api/v1/brands/default/versions/3/restore
```
