---
title: Portals
description: A branded front door onto chosen collections and brand guidelines, for people outside the team.
---

A portal is a press kit, a partner hub or a retailer page: the brand pages and
collections you pick, under your logo and colors, at `/p/{address}` or a domain
of its own. Make one from **Portals** in the sidebar, with
`POST /api/v1/portals`, or have an agent make one with `create_portal` (open to
the workspace's members, to whoever has its password, or to anyone, with its
logo and colors) and change one with `update_portal`. It
takes write on the workspace, and sharing rights on each collection. A portal
shows at least one collection or one brand.

## What shows

Only what may be used: approved, unexpired, out of embargo, and a stack's
current version. The same rule as `/a/{id}`, so an asset that expires, gets
archived or is replaced by a new version leaves every portal the moment that
happens. Nobody has to remember to take it down.

Collections make the portal's **Assets** view (`?view=assets`), where
visitors search, filter by collection, look closer, and download. To show a
collection inside the guidelines instead, add a Collection section to a
brand page.

## Brand pages

A portal publishes the [brand pages](/guides/canon#brand-pages) of each brand
it carries, in its own theme: the tree of pages in a nav column, the page, and
a switch between brands. Brands go in by slug, in order
(`"brands": ["default", "sub-brand"]`).

Visitors read a brand's **latest publish, never the draft**. Edit as much as
you like: nothing reaches a portal until someone publishes, and a brand that
was never published shows nothing at all. The Portals page says so beside
each brand: the version visitors read and when it was published, or "not
published". Publishing answers with the portals now showing the brand.

A rule's assets show by the same rule as everything else, so a draft logo
stays in the library, and every asset is signed for the visitor.

| Address | What it opens |
|---|---|
| `/p/{address}` | The first brand's first page |
| `/p/{address}/{page}` | A page of the first brand |
| `/p/{address}/{brand}/{page}` | A page of another brand |
| `?view=updates` | What's new: each publish, its note, and what it changed |
| `?view=book` | Every page in order, to read through or print |

Moving a page in the tree never changes its address, and a renamed page's old
slug leads to its new one. Pages show when they were last updated, and the
footer when the brand was last published. `/` opens search, across the pages,
the rules and the portal's assets.

The API serves the same, behind the same door as the portal:
`GET /api/v1/portal/{address}/site?path=`, `/search?q=` and `/updates?brand=`.
`GET /api/v1/portal/{address}/brands/{slug}` still answers with the brand's
rules, now as published.

Publishing and sharing are two steps: a publish is the version readers get,
a portal the address they read it at, with who gets in and its look. One brand
can be on several portals, and a portal can show several brands. A brand no
portal shows yet gets one in the same step as its publish, if you like: the
publish dialog offers a portal named for the brand, open to the workspace's
members or to anyone with the address. Publishing from the builder with no
portal also links to a new one with the brand already picked.

A portal's menu on the Portals page takes it offline at once (its address
says it is closed, and nothing about it is lost), brings it back, or deletes
it. Over the API, `POST /api/v1/portals/{id}/close` takes it offline and
`PATCH` with `expiresAt: null` reopens it.

## Who reads what

A page or a section can be for fewer readers than the whole portal:

| Audience | Who reads it |
|---|---|
| `everyone` | Anyone the portal lets in (the default) |
| `partners` | Visitors let in by the password or an approved request, and members |
| `members` | People signed in with access to the workspace |

What is above a visitor is left out before anything is sent or signed. A
locked page still shows in the nav, with a lock and a way in: **Ask for
access** or **Sign in**. A public portal carrying partner or member pages
takes access requests too. Set the audience on a page or a section, in the
app or with `save_page` and `edit_page`.

## Downloads

Images download as renditions made for a purpose, not the raw original:

| Preset | What they get |
|---|---|
| Web | JPEG, 1920 px wide |
| Social | JPEG, 1080 px square |
| Story | JPEG, 1080 × 1920 |
| Print | JPEG, full size |
| PNG | Full size, keeps transparency |
| Original | The file as uploaded, with its metadata written in |

Web, Print and Social unless you pick others. Anything that isn't an image (a
PDF, a video, a font) downloads as itself. Each file's card lists its presets,
a download each.

A file shown only ([its rights](/guides/assets#shown-not-handed-out): a
licensed photo, a foundry's typeface) has none: visitors see it, and a
portal's pages still set its typeface, but nothing offers it to take. A portal
that lets in more than the workspace says so where its door is set.

On each card and beside a file's downloads, **Can I use this?** asks its rights for a use, a
country and a day ([the check](/guides/check), behind the portal's door:
`POST /api/v1/portal/{slug}/check`). It only answers about files the portal
shows, and counts in Insights as the portal's.

The portal's header names what it shows: **Guidelines** (each brand by its
name, when it carries several), **Assets**, **What's new** (`?view=updates`)
and **Contact**, the footer's feedback address when one is set.

## Who gets in

| Access | Who |
|---|---|
| Anyone with the address | Everyone. Search engines are asked to stay out, unless it is [listed](#search-engines). |
| Whoever has the password | Ten wrong guesses in ten minutes, then it waits. |
| People in this workspace | Signed in, with access to the workspace. |

A portal can close on a date: after it, the address answers `410`.

On the last two, anyone else can **ask for access**: their email, a name, and
a note. The workspace's admins get an email. Approve, and they get a link of
their own by email (and one to copy, if email is off), good for 90 days or
until the portal closes. Remove the request to take it back.

## Look

A logo (an approved image from the library), an accent color for buttons and
links, and a header background. They dress the portal's header, its door and
its Assets view; the pages wear their brand's own theme.

A portal made for a brand wears its look: an empty logo is the mark of the
first brand it shows (its logo rule named mark, icon or symbol, else its first
logo with an image) and an empty accent its `color.primary` (else its first
color), from the release visitors read, so the portal follows the brand as it
changes. The form shows them as "From" and the brand's name. Set either to override it, for
a white-label portal. With no brand to borrow from, the organization's own.

A brand's Sharing tab makes a portal for it in the same form, the brand
picked, and lists the portals showing it.

## Around the pages

The portal's `site` holds what sits around the pages, set under **Around the
pages** or in the API, where it replaces the whole set:

```json
{
  "site": {
    "quick": [
      { "label": "Logo pack", "asset": "7b1d0e3c-..." },
      { "label": "Colors", "page": "color" },
      { "label": "Press office", "href": "mailto:press@example.com" }
    ],
    "footer": {
      "text": "Questions about the brand: **brand@example.com**",
      "links": [{ "label": "Newsroom", "href": "https://example.com/news" }],
      "credit": "Design by Studio North",
      "feedback": "mailto:brand@example.com"
    },
    "terms": "For editorial use about Example only.",
    "listed": true
  }
}
```

- **Quick grab**: up to six links pinned in the header. Each opens a page (of
  `brand`, or the first), downloads an asset, signed for the visitor while it
  may be used, or goes to an address.
- **Footer**: Markdown, up to eight links, a credit line and where feedback
  goes. The footer also says when the brand was last updated, and links to
  What's new.
- **Terms**: Markdown visitors accept once, before their first download. It
  is a courtesy, not a lock: each file is still signed on its own.

Links go to `https://`, `mailto:` or a `/path`, never a script.

## Languages

A brand's theme lists the languages its pages come in
(`"languages": [{ "code": "en", "label": "English" }, { "code": "ar", "label": "العربية" }]`);
the first is the one they are written in. Pages and sections carry their words
in the others under `translations`, and a visitor who picks one (`?lang=ar`)
reads it field by field, falling back to the first language where a
translation is missing. A brand with a context named after the language (`ar`)
resolves its rules for it too.

Right-to-left languages turn the whole page, and fonts whose rule names the
language's script lead the type.

## Print and the book

**Print this page** prints the page without the portal around it: colors as
they are, every tab's content under its name, and the address of each link
that leaves the portal. **Print the book** prints every page the visitor may read, in order, with
a table of contents first. Both are the browser's print, so saving a PDF works
the same way.

## Search engines

Portals are kept out of search: every page asks not to be indexed, and each
portal's `robots.txt` turns crawlers away. A public portal can opt in with
**Let search engines list it** (`"listed": true`): its pages may be indexed,
titled after the page and its brand, with the page's lede as the description
and its cover as the preview image. A portal behind a password or for members
is never listed.

## BrandHub

A server with `HUB_URL` set runs BrandHub. Every published brand is on it at
`{organization}/{brand}`, **private** by default: only people who can read its
workspace see it, signed in. **Make public** on the brand's Sharing tab or the Brands page (or
`PATCH /api/v1/brands/{slug}/hub` with `"visibility": "public"`, or the
`set_brand_hub` tool) and anyone, and any agent, reads it. Either way BrandHub
shows the brand's latest publish, with only the assets that may be used, so a
brand goes public once it has been published, and later publishes show there
as they happen.

A brand needs no portal to be on BrandHub. When a portal shows it, BrandHub
links that portal as its guidelines: its first public one, or the one picked
on its row of the brand's Sharing tab, where every portal showing the brand
is listed with who gets in and the requests waiting (`"portal"`).

With a host of its own (`HUB_URL=https://hub.example.com`), BrandHub serves
public brands there, to anyone. When that host sits beside the app's
(`hub.example.com` and `app.example.com`), the app's sign-in reaches it: the
session cookie is set for `example.com`, and people signed in see their
private brands there too. On a host that shares no domain with the app's,
they find them on the app's own address, at `/hub`. BrandHub, at the end of
the app's sidebar, opens whichever of the two that is, in a new tab.

A brand's address is `{org}/{brand}`, and `@{n}` pins a version:

| Address | What it is |
|---|---|
| `/{org}/{brand}` | Its page: colors, type, logos, voice, terms, and a link to its portal |
| `/{org}/{brand}/llms.txt` | The brand in words, for an agent |
| `/{org}/{brand}/brand.json` | The brand as an AdCP brand.json: logos, colors, fonts, voice, don'ts ([the mapping](/guides/brand-as-code/brand-json)) |
| `/{org}/{brand}/rules.json` | Every rule, its files as URLs signed for a day |
| `/{org}/{brand}/tokens?format=css` | Design tokens, in any format of `GET /api/v1/brand/tokens` ([formats](/guides/brand-as-code/standards)) |
| `/{org}/{brand}/badge.svg` | A README badge with the release that is live ("brand @4"), not counted as a read |
| `/index.json?q=` | The public brands, or what a search finds |
| `/llms.txt` | How an agent finds a brand here, and the list |

The last six answer for public brands only. Anyone may make public a brand
of any name, so a page names who has it. An organization with a verified
[domain](#a-domain-of-its-own) or a verified GitHub account gets a badge with
it; any other public brand is a community one, says so, and is kept out of
search engines (`noindex` on its page, `X-Robots-Tag: noindex` on its files,
and left out of the sitemap).

### Verified by GitHub

Beside a domain, an organization can prove it runs a GitHub organization, in
Settings, **BrandHub** (`POST /api/v1/github-orgs`), which opens on whether
its listings are verified and by which domains and accounts. Put the file it names,
`artbucket-verification.txt` holding the token shown, in the account's
`.github` repository on its default branch, then **Check now**
(`POST /api/v1/github-orgs/{login}/verify`). The server reads it from
`raw.githubusercontent.com`, following no redirect. Once proved, the
organization's listings show as verified by `github.com/{login}`. Like a
domain, one left unproved for 7 days may be named by another organization.

### Report, claim and delist

Every public listing has **Report**: anyone, signed in or not, says what is
wrong (`POST /api/v1/hub/{org}/{brand}/reports`: a reason, a note, and a way
to reach them if they want). A community listing also has **It's my brand**:
an admin of an organization that proved a domain or a GitHub account claims
it (`POST /api/v1/hub/{org}/{brand}/claims`), and the claim names what they
proved and their email. Nothing of either is public, and no IP address is
kept: an address only counts toward a few reports an hour.

Both land in the listing's organization's Settings, **BrandHub**
(`GET /api/v1/hub/reports`), where its admins resolve them, reach a claimant
and hand the brand over (the claimant can start from it, below), or **Take off
BrandHub**, which makes the brand private (`PATCH /api/v1/hub/reports/{id}`
with `"delist": true`). The organization can always take its own brand off
BrandHub the same way on the Brands page.

### Claim by domain

A public listing may name its brand's domain (its Settings, or its
[brand.json](/guides/adcp)). An organization that verifies that domain, a name
under it (`brand.acme.com` proves `acme.com`) or one above it, is offered the
listings of other organizations that never proved it: on the Brands page ("Claim
Test on BrandHub has your domain acme.com: make it yours") and in Settings,
**BrandHub** (`GET /api/v1/hub/offers`). Never across a public suffix: a name
under a zone that hands out names to others (`acme.github.io`, `acme.vercel.app`,
from the Public Suffix List) proves itself and the names under it, not the zone
or a sibling. Such a listing reads **Unclaimed · Is
this yours? Prove acme.com to claim it** when its organization proved nothing.

**Make it yours** (`POST /api/v1/hub/offers/{org}/{brand}`, `{"slug"}` to name
it otherwise) starts a brand in your workspace from the listing's release, as
**Start from this brand** does, with its domain, released and public. The
listing goes private, its page and files answer `308` to yours for good, and it
can't be made public again; its organization keeps its brand, finds a resolved
claim in Settings, **BrandHub**, and an entry in its audit log. No brand moves
between organizations. **Not ours** (`DELETE` on the same path) stops the offer.
Anything else, a listing with no domain or one whose organization proved it,
goes through **It's my brand** above.

Whoever runs the server reads every report in the database, and can delist a
brand for good: while `hub_delisted` holds a reason, the brand is off BrandHub
and its organization can't make it public again, which says why.

```sql
-- Open reports and claims, newest first
select r.created_at, r.kind, r.reason, r.note, r.contact, r.proof, o.slug as org, b.slug as brand
from hub_reports r
join brands b on b.id = r.brand_id
join workspaces w on w.id = b.workspace_id
join organizations o on o.id = w.organization_id
where r.status = 'open'
order by r.created_at desc;

-- Delist acme/firefox (setting null lists it again: its organization may then make it public)
update brands set visibility = 'private', hub_delisted = 'A trademark complaint from its owner'
where slug = 'firefox' and workspace_id in (select w.id from workspaces w join organizations o on o.id = w.organization_id where o.slug = 'acme');
```

### Start from this brand

Signed in, **Start from this brand** on a public listing makes a new brand in
the workspace you have open from that release: its rules, its theme, the pages
and sections anyone may read (none hidden, or for partners or members), and
the files they use that may be delivered, copied into your library (the same
bytes, stored once). The new brand is yours to edit, with a history of its
own, and keeps where it came from as `from`. An agent does the same with
`create_brand` and `from: "rust-lang/rust@12"`, or `POST /api/v1/brands` with
`{"name": "...", "from": "rust-lang/rust@12"}`; leave out `@12` for the
latest release. A claimant a listing is handed over to starts from it the
same way.

### Pulls, follows, trending and collections

A listing's **pulls** are its files (`brand.json`, `llms.txt`, `tokens`) read
in the last 30 days, the same reads [Insights](/guides/insights) counts. Its
card, its page and the brand's Overview show them ("1.2k pulls"), and
`index.json` and `GET /api/v1/brands/{slug}/hub` carry them as `pulls`.

Someone signed in can **Follow** a public listing
(`PUT /api/v1/hub/{org}/{brand}/follow`, `DELETE` to stop): BrandHub's front
page then has a **Following** tab with the brands they follow. **Trending
this week**, the order it opens in, sorts by pulls in the last 7 days. A
community listing's card says it may not come from the brand's owner, and
the front page ends with a domain box for the public Brand Agent Score.

Curated collections, like "Open-source project brands", are the server
operator's: each is a row in `hub_collections`, listing public brands as
`{org}/{brand}` in order, and shows as a section on BrandHub's front page
(lowest `position` first; a brand that isn't public is left out).

```sql
insert into hub_collections (slug, title, description, position, brands)
values ('open-source', 'Open-source project brands', 'Projects that share their brand in the open.', 0,
        array['blender/blender', 'mozilla/firefox', 'rust-lang/rust']);
```

### A public Brand Agent Score

`/score` on BrandHub checks any domain the way an agent would find the brand
there, for anyone, and scores it out of 100 on the scale of the Brand Agent
Score in the app: colors (15), typefaces (15), a logo with its file (20) and a
voice (15), read from the brand's rules; rules a machine reads (15), a
`brand.json` its `llms.txt` links or at `/.well-known/brand.json`, or a public
BrandHub listing of an organization that verified the domain; an `llms.txt` at its root (10);
design tokens (5); and an MCP server its `llms.txt` names that answers (5).
The report lists what raises the score, and offers to list the brand here.

A stranger sets it off, so it is bounded: at most four fetches per domain,
each to public addresses only, 256 KB and 6 seconds at most; a report is kept
five minutes; and each address may ask ten times in ten minutes, everyone
together two hundred. The address is never stored.

## A subdomain for every portal

With `PORTAL_DOMAIN` set, say `portals.example.com`, every portal
answers at `{address}.portals.example.com`, with nothing to claim or prove:
the server owns the domain. That is the address the app shows and copies,
but for a **members** portal, which stays at `/p/{address}` (sessions belong
to the app's own address), and a portal with a verified domain of its own.

`/p/{address}` redirects there for good, so links shared before keep working.
Renaming a portal keeps its old address, `/p/` and subdomain alike, leading
to the new one, and nobody else may take it while the portal stands. It keeps
its last five; an older one, and every one when the portal is deleted, is
free again. While you type an address, the form says whether it is
free (`GET /api/v1/portals/address?slug=...`). Some addresses are kept
for the service, like `www`, `api` or `mail`, and so is any with dashes as its
third and fourth characters (`xn--`, the shape of a look-alike name).

It takes a wildcard DNS record, `*.portals.example.com`, pointing at the
server, and a wildcard certificate, or on-demand TLS as below:
`/api/v1/domains/check` answers `200` for a subdomain whose portal exists.
On a domain of its own rather than a subdomain of the app's, a portal can't
touch the app's cookies; list it on the [Public Suffix
List](https://publicsuffix.org) too, so portals can't touch each other's.

## A domain of its own

Domains are the organization's, added in Settings, Domains, like
`press.example.com`. Each shows a TXT record to add at your DNS host, which
proves the domain is yours:

```
_artbucket-challenge.press.example.com  TXT  artbucket-3f2a...
```

Point the domain itself at your server (a `CNAME` to its host name, or an `A`
record), then **Check now**. With `DOMAIN_TARGET` set, the exact `CNAME` is
shown, and checked along with the TXT record; at a zone's apex, where a CNAME
can't go, an ALIAS or flattened record to the same target works.

A verified domain serves nothing until you choose what it is for. Turn on
**Use for the app** beside it in Settings, Domains, and the whole app answers
there, with links in email pointing there too (one domain per organization,
off when verified). Or pick it in a portal: **Domain of its own** lists the
organization's verified domains, and the domain then serves that portal and
nothing else of the app. A domain the app used stops serving it when a portal
takes it.
Removing a domain in Settings sends its portal back to `/p/{address}`.
How many domains an organization may add is the operator's `domains`
[limit](/configuration/limits).

### TLS

The server speaks plain HTTP behind a reverse proxy. For certificates on
domains you don't know in advance, let the proxy ask the app first. With
[Caddy](https://caddyserver.com/docs/automatic-https#on-demand-tls):

```
{
  on_demand_tls {
    ask http://artbucket:3000/api/v1/domains/check
  }
}

assets.example.com {
  reverse_proxy artbucket:3000
}

https:// {
  tls {
    on_demand
  }
  reverse_proxy artbucket:3000
}
```

`GET /api/v1/domains/check?domain=...` answers `200` only for a verified
domain, so nobody can make your server request certificates for names it
doesn't serve.

<Note>
A **members** portal on its own domain can't see who is signed in: sessions
belong to the app's own address. Give it a password, or keep it at `/p/`.
</Note>
