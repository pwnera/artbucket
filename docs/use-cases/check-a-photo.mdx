title: Check whether a photo may be used
sidebarTitle: Check a photo
description: Record a license once, then get a yes or no for a channel, a country and a date, with what to use instead.
---

Someone wants to run a stock photo in a paid social ad in Germany next month.
You need to know whether the license covers that, without digging out the
contract. Record the license on the asset once; after that anyone, including
an AI tool, can ask and get a plain answer.

## Record the license

1. Open the photo and expand its **Rights** fold.
2. Fill in what the license says:
   - **License**: the agency and the terms, in words.
   - **Territories**: the countries it covers, as two-letter codes (`DE`,
     `AT`). Empty means everywhere.
   - **Channels**: where it may run. The usual ones are `web`, `social`,
     `paid-social`, `email`, `print`, `out-of-home`, `broadcast` and
     `editorial`. Empty means anywhere.
   - **Not before**: the first day it may be used, for an embargo.
   - **Last day of use**: when the license ends.
   - **Model release**: **Signed**, **Missing: editorial use only**, or **Not
     needed** when nobody recognizable is in it.
   - **Downloads**: **Shown only** if people outside the team may see it but
     not take the file.
3. In the **Provenance** fold, set where it came from: **Licensed** for stock.

## Ask before you use it

1. Open the asset and expand **Can I use this?**.
2. Fill in **Where** (the channel), **Territory** and **When**. Leave **When**
   empty for today.
3. Choose **Check**.

The answer is **Yes, you can use it** or **Not for this use**, with the
reasons. When the asset was replaced, or the brand has a version for that
context, **Use instead** names the file to use.

## What a refusal means

| It says | In plain words |
|---|---|
| Not approved, archived or deleted | The file isn't cleared for use at all |
| Replaced | A newer file took its place; use that one |
| Before its embargo, or after its last day | The dates don't cover your use |
| Outside its territories or channels | The license doesn't cover that country or that kind of use |
| No model release | People are in it without a signed release; only `editorial` use is allowed |
| A variant for this context | The brand has a version for, say, dark backgrounds; use that |

If you leave out the country or the channel and the license limits it, the
answer mentions the limit without refusing. Fill both in to settle it.

Partners ask the same question on a portal: each file there has **Can I use
this?**. AI tools ask it through MCP before they hand a file out.

## Hear about licenses before they end

This takes a project admin.

1. Open **Automations** in the sidebar, then **From a recipe**, and pick
   **Licenses ending, to legal**.
2. Add the addresses to tell and save with **Make it**. **Send a test** shows
   what the email looks like.

From then on, each approved asset's license sends one email 30 days before its
last day. **Fixes** lists the licenses ending in the next 30 days too, and the
Monday digest those ending in the next 14.

## Keep a record

**Insights**, **Use checks** lists every answer given, to people and to
agents, with the reasons and whether the replacement was taken. **Export
CSV** saves them.
