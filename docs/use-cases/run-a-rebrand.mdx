---
title: Run a rebrand
description: Change colors, type, logo and pages as a draft, get them reviewed, and release them on one day without breaking what is live.
---

The new colors and the redrawn logo launch on the 1st. Until then the old
brand stays live for partners, the website and public AI tools. On the day,
one release switches them over, and you can see who is still on the old one.

## Before launch day

1. Edit the brand. Change the colors and fonts on the **Rules** tab, the look
   in **Theme**, and the pages through **Edit**. All of it is a draft: portals
   and BrandHub keep showing the current release. AI tools your team connected
   over MCP read the rules as they stand, draft included, so tell the team
   when you start.
2. Upload the new logo as a new version of the old one, with **New version**
   and **Upload as a draft** in the asset's **Versions** fold. A draft stays
   out of the library and off every link.
3. Ask for review. Reviewers open the brand's **Guidelines** tab and leave
   notes with **Comments**, on a page or a section. Anyone with Contributor
   access can comment.

## On launch day

1. Approve the new logo: open the draft version and choose **Approve**. From
   that moment every `/c/` link and embed shows it.
2. Point the logo rule at it: on **Rules**, open the logo rule's details and
   pick the new version under **Assets**.
3. Choose **Release** in the brand's header. The form shows every change since
   the live release, line by line: a color's value before and after, each page
   edited. It also shows the Brand Agent Score before and after, and links to
   comments still open.
4. Write the **Note for readers**: what changed and why. Portals show it under
   What's new.
5. Choose **Release**.

## What changes at once

- Portals and BrandHub show the new release.
- AI tools that read the brand from BrandHub, its brand.json or its
  `llms.txt` get the new colors, fonts and logo on their next question.
- The PowerPoint and Word templates on **Brand files** are made from the new
  release.
- Design tokens and `/c/` links pick up the change without a deploy.

## Sites that need a rebuild

A website that copies the colors into its code at build time keeps the old
ones until it is built again. Give each such site a deploy hook: in **Sites**,
open the site and fill in **Rebuild on release**. Each release then starts a
build. Your developers can also use the **Rebuild a site on each release**
recipe in **Automations** to call their CI.

## After launch

Open **Fixes** a few days later. Each site or agent still loading an old file
is a row; **Notify** emails whoever answers for it, with links that always lead
to the current version. The brand's **Insights** shows how much of its traffic
is on the latest release.

## If you need to go back

Every edit is kept as a version of the brand. To bring an earlier one back,
ask a developer to run `artbucket restore <version> --brand <slug>`, or ask a
connected AI tool to restore it. The restore is a new draft: release it to put
it live. On the logo, **Make current** on the old version in **Versions**
switches the links back.
