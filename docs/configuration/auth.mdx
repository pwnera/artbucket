People sign in with an email and a password, with the server's OpenID Connect
provider, or with their organization's own. Single sign-on is free here, and
stays free.

## Who may make an account

By default (`SIGNUP=invite`):

- the first account on a fresh server, which is the admin of everything
- anyone holding an invitation link, which an admin makes on Team
- anyone the OIDC provider vouches for, who arrives with no access until an
  admin gives them some
- anyone at an organization's verified email domain its own provider vouches
  for, who joins that organization able to read

`SIGNUP=open` lets anyone make an account from the sign-in page. Each new
account gets an organization of its own, with a first workspace, and is its
admin. Invitations still work: someone signing up from one joins where it
points instead.

### A check against scripted sign-ups

With sign-up open, making an account with a password, and sending its email
code, can take a check that a script can't pass for free. Two kinds:

- **Cloudflare Turnstile**, at `APP_URL`'s address. Set a
  [Turnstile](https://developers.cloudflare.com/turnstile/) widget's keys, with
  `APP_URL`'s host among its hostnames:

  ```bash
  TURNSTILE_SITE_KEY=0x4AAAAAAA...
  TURNSTILE_SECRET_KEY=0x4AAAAAAA...
  ```

  The sign-up form (first run and invitations included) shows the widget above
  its button, and the server verifies its token with Cloudflare before the
  account is made.
- **A proof of work**, built in: the browser spends a second or so finding a
  number the server asked for (the [ALTCHA](https://altcha.org) protocol),
  while the form is filled in, with "Checking you're not a bot" under it until
  it is done. Nothing third party, nothing loaded from elsewhere, so it works
  on any domain.

Which one a sign-up takes depends on where the app is open:

| | `APP_URL` | An organization's own domain for the app |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `TURNSTILE_*` set | Turnstile | proof of work |
| `SIGNUP_CAPTCHA=pow` alone | proof of work | proof of work |
| neither | no check | no check |

A Turnstile widget only runs on the hostnames it lists, and organizations add
their domains as they go: they get the proof of work instead. Sending a new
email code takes the same check, out of sight unless Turnstile asks for a
click. Signing in, resetting a password, and signing up through Google, the
OIDC provider or an organization's own are not checked. `GET /api/v1/me`
says which check the host asked takes (`auth.captcha`).

## Email domains

An organization proves the domains its people have their email at in Settings,
Email domains (`/api/v1/email-domains`): a TXT record at
`_artbucket-challenge.acme.com` holding the token shown, then Check now. One
organization holds a domain; a claim left unproved for seven days can be taken
by another. Public suffixes (`co.uk`, `github.io`) are refused. These are not
the custom domains the app and portals answer at, and no limit counts them.

### Joining by domain

A proved domain can be opened: "Anyone at acme.com can join, able to read".
Whoever then signs up, or signs in, with an address at exactly that domain is
offered to join the organization once the email code has confirmed the
address: on the welcome page, beside starting an organization of their own,
or in a banner across the app for an account made before. Not now is kept.
With sign-up closed, such an address may still make an account: the offer is
what it is for.

It is refused for domains that give addresses to the public (gmail.com,
orange.fr, comcast.net: some 14,000 of them), for a domain single sign-on
covers, whose people join through the provider, and on a server without its
own email (`EMAIL_*`), where nothing confirms the address. Joining by domain
doesn't remove anyone who leaves the company: an admin does, or requires
single sign-on.

## Single sign-on

### An organization's own

Each organization can bring its own provider in Settings, Single sign-on
(`/api/v1/sso`), with no restart and nothing in the environment. This is the
one for a server shared by several organizations. An organization admin:

1. registers a web app with the provider (Okta, Entra ID, Google Workspace, or
   any OpenID Connect one), with the redirect URIs the page shows:
   `{APP_URL}/api/auth/sso/callback/{organization id}`, and the same path on
   each domain the organization uses for the app (Settings, Domains), since
   signing in there comes back there
2. saves its issuer URL, client ID and client secret, and the email domain its
   people use, e.g. `acme.com`. The endpoints are read from the issuer's
   discovery document then, and must be public https addresses
3. proves the domain: a TXT record at `_artbucket-challenge.acme.com`
   holding the token shown, then Check now. The domain is one of the
   organization's email domains: one proved there already is proved here

From then on the sign-in page takes anyone at that domain, or under it, to
the provider once they type their email. The first time, they join the
organization able to read; an admin raises their access in Team. An account
made before, with a password, joins at its next sign-in through the provider.
Signing up with a password at the domain is not on offer: the sign-in page
takes the address to the provider instead, so nobody there ends up in an
organization of their own by mistake.
The provider vouches for its own domain only: an address anywhere else is
refused. One domain belongs to one organization; a claim left unproved for
seven days can be taken by another. Turning it off keeps everyone's account
and access: they sign in with a password, reset by email.

#### Requiring it

Once the domain is proved, Require single sign-on (`PATCH /api/v1/sso` with
`{"required": true}`) holds everyone at the domain to the provider: password
sign-in sends them there, and no password reset goes out, so leaving the
provider means leaving Artbucket. Requiring it signs out everyone at the domain
at once. The organization's admins are the exception: they keep their password,
so a provider that breaks later (a rotated secret, a changed app) never locks
the organization out of fixing it. Members at other domains, an agency's or a
freelancer's, are never held to it.

### The server's

For a server with one company on it, set all three, for Okta, Entra ID, Google
Workspace, Keycloak, Authentik or any other OpenID Connect provider, internal
ones included:

```bash
OIDC_ISSUER=https://accounts.example.com
OIDC_CLIENT_ID=...
OIDC_CLIENT_SECRET=...
OIDC_NAME="Acme SSO"
```

Register `{APP_URL}/api/auth/callback/oidc` as the redirect URI with the
provider, and `/api/auth/callback/oidc` on any organization's domain used for
the app where people sign in with it.

## Email

Off by default. Email sends invitations, share links and password resets
("Forgot your password?" appears once some email can go out). Turn it on for
the whole server:

```bash
EMAIL_PROVIDER=resend
EMAIL_FROM="Acme Assets <assets@acme.example>"
EMAIL_API_KEY=re_...
```

Set this way, email is the server's alone: every organization sends through
it, none can change the sender, the key or the provider, and Settings, Email is
not offered (`PATCH /api/v1/settings/email` answers `403`). A hosted server
sends from its own domain with its own account, and no organization borrows it
to send as someone else.

Without it, each organization can turn on its own in Settings, Email.
Providers are HTTP APIs, no SMTP: `resend`, `postmark`, `sendgrid`, and
`console`, which prints to the log. A message that fails never fails what sent
it: the link is still shown, and the failure is in the audit log.

## Confirming addresses

With the server's email set, a new account proves its address before it signs
in: signing up mails a six-digit code, good for ten minutes and five tries, and
the account signs in once it is entered. An account that never confirmed gets
a fresh code the next time it signs in with its password, so accounts made
before this confirm once too. Single sign-on needs no code: the provider
vouched for the address. Without the server's email there is nothing to send
a code from before an account has an organization, so none is asked.
