People sign in with an email and a password, with the server's OpenID Connect
provider, or with their organization's own. Single sign-on is free here, and
stays free.

## Who may make an account

By default (`SIGNUP=invite`):

- the first account on a fresh server, which is the admin of everything
- anyone holding an invitation link, which an admin makes on Team
- anyone the OIDC provider vouches for, who arrives with no access until an
  admin gives them some
- anyone at an organization's verified email domain its own provider vouches
  for, who joins that organization able to read

`SIGNUP=open` lets anyone make an account from the sign-in page. Each new
account gets an organization of its own, with a first workspace, and is its
admin. Invitations still work: someone signing up from one joins where it
points instead.

## Single sign-on

### An organization's own

Each organization can bring its own provider in Settings, Single sign-on
(`/api/v1/sso`), with no restart and nothing in the environment. This is the
one for a server shared by several organizations. An organization admin:

1. registers a web app with the provider (Okta, Entra ID, Google Workspace, or
   any OpenID Connect one), with the redirect URI the page shows:
   `{APP_URL}/api/auth/sso/callback/{organization id}`
2. saves its issuer URL, client ID and client secret, and the email domain its
   people use, e.g. `acme.com`. The endpoints are read from the issuer's
   discovery document then, and must be public https addresses
3. proves the domain: a TXT record at `_artbucket-challenge.acme.com`
   holding the token shown, then Check now

From then on "Sign in with SSO" on the sign-in page takes anyone at that
domain, or under it, to the provider. The first time, they join the
organization able to read; an admin raises their access in Team. An account
made before, with a password, joins at its next sign-in through the provider.
The provider vouches for its own domain only: an address anywhere else is
refused. One domain belongs to one organization; a claim left unproved for
seven days can be taken by another. Turning it off keeps everyone's account
and access: they sign in with a password, reset by email.

### The server's

For a server with one company on it, set all three, for Okta, Entra ID, Google
Workspace, Keycloak, Authentik or any other OpenID Connect provider, internal
ones included:

```bash
OIDC_ISSUER=https://accounts.example.com
OIDC_CLIENT_ID=...
OIDC_CLIENT_SECRET=...
OIDC_NAME="Acme SSO"
```

Register `{APP_URL}/api/auth/callback/oidc` as the redirect URI with the
provider.

## Email

Off by default. Email sends invitations, share links and password resets
("Forgot your password?" appears once some email can go out). Turn it on for
the whole server:

```bash
EMAIL_PROVIDER=resend
EMAIL_FROM="Acme Assets <assets@acme.example>"
EMAIL_API_KEY=re_...
```

Set this way, email is the server's alone: every organization sends through
it, none can change the sender, the key or the provider, and Settings, Email is
not offered (`PATCH /api/v1/settings/email` answers `403`). A hosted server
sends from its own domain with its own account, and no organization borrows it
to send as someone else.

Without it, each organization can turn on its own in Settings, Email.
Providers are HTTP APIs, no SMTP: `resend`, `postmark`, `sendgrid`, and
`console`, which prints to the log. A message that fails never fails what sent
it: the link is still shown, and the failure is in the audit log.

## Confirming addresses

With the server's email set, a new account proves its address before it signs
in: signing up mails a six-digit code, good for ten minutes and five tries, and
the account signs in once it is entered. An account that never confirmed gets
a fresh code the next time it signs in with its password, so accounts made
before this confirm once too. Single sign-on needs no code: the provider
vouched for the address. Without the server's email there is nothing to send
a code from before an account has an organization, so none is asked.
